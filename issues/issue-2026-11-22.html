AI Automation Daily - November 22, 2026

Today's theme: the price-per-task floor keeps dropping, and the savings now come from where you run each step, not just which model you pick. Five fresh, practical findings you can put to work this week.

1. Gemini 4 Neon makes mid-tier the permanent value champion

Google's Gemini 4 Neon (released Nov 18) is priced at $3 in / $15 out per million tokens with cached input at $0.15 - a 95% cache cut - and a 1,048,576-token context window. It runs about 110 tokens/sec versus 57 for Carbon, and by vendor numbers delivers roughly 32% lower cost per task than Carbon while scoring higher on the intelligence index. It landed day-one support in Cursor, Copilot and LangChain, so switching stacks needs no integration work. Practical move: for lead qualification, invoice parsing and support triage, make Neon your default workhorse - its pricing is permanent, unlike Carbon's intro rate that doubles to $4/$20 on Dec 1. Renegotiate any inference contract priced on the old rate card.

2. GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol cut agent prices 20-60%

OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on Nov 17. Astra added +5.2 points on Agents' Last Exam at less than one-third the price of its predecessor and +5.5 over Claude Fable 5.2 at about 5% of the cost; it lists at $8/$40 per million with cached input at $0.40 (down 60%). Sol sits at $2/$10. With three flagships landing in three days and reported price cuts of 20-600%, any pipeline still billed at old rates deserves a recalculation. Practical move: reprice your high-volume automations and route the cheap, frequent steps to Sol while reserving Astra for the few steps that truly need frontier reasoning.

3. Google hands Kubernetes-grade agent infrastructure to the CNCF - free

On Nov 20 Google donated Agent Substrate, gVisor and OpenRL to the CNCF. Google estimates agents idle 90-95% of the time just holding state; Agent Substrate splits state from compute and recovers in under 500 ms, while gVisor adds user-space kernel isolation. Practical move: you now have neutral, open-source components for resilient agent deployments without a vendor bill - a real saving for anyone running long-lived agents that currently pay for idle infrastructure. Pair this with least-privilege permissions rather than screen-scraping, which is increasingly blocked (Signal moved to block OS-agent screenshots on iOS 27 and Android in Nov).

4. Perplexity Orchestrator API: provider failover with no markup

Perplexity launched the Orchestrator API on Nov 19: one endpoint spanning OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI and open-weight models, with same-model failover that swaps the provider (for example Claude to AWS Bedrock) when one goes down. Per the company, failover traffic beat every single provider on their Oct 4 - Nov 2 data, with no platform markup. Practical move: eliminate single-vendor outage risk in your automation with a single integration and one bill - especially valuable if a failed API call currently stops your whole workflow and forces a manual restart.

5. OpenVorld and the local-first shift: run a free agent crew on keys you already pay for

Launched Nov 21, OpenVorld is a local-first, MIT-licensed harness that turns a project folder into a pixel-art "vorld" where a crew of agents (Claude Code, Codex, or any OpenAI-compatible API including Ollama and LM Studio) work with named roles, hand off via @mentions, and must request a "writ" before touching a folder, command or website. No account, no subscription - you pay only your own providers. It rides a broader local-first wave: a Dev.to/Cumulus analysis puts local inference at roughly $0.02 per million tokens in electricity versus $0.50-$15 through cloud APIs, with 7B-13B models on consumer hardware handling most small-business automation. Practical move: trial a local crew for repetitive, privacy-sensitive jobs (email triage, document summarization), but keep frontier cloud models for deep multi-step reasoning.

Automation tip of the day: price the task, not the token. Agent workflows don't make one call - they plan, call tools, re-read context and retry. The median agentic outcome costs about $0.47 (vs ~$0.02 for a single chat), and letting a model "think harder" can double the bill by generating far more output tokens. Three levers: (1) route each step to the cheapest model that clears the bar (Sol or Neon for the bulk, Astra only where needed); (2) use effort settings and caching - on-demand tool schemas and cached input at a fraction of base price; (3) set a per-run spend cap and a kill switch so a retry loop cannot quietly drain the budget. Cheaper tokens can produce more expensive tasks if you skip this.
