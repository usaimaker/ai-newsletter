AI Automation Daily — August 13, 2026

Today's news has an unusually clear shape. The cheap lane got much cheaper, the open lane gained a serious new entrant, and the compliance lane moved quietly under everyone's feet. Three of the five items below cut what you pay per task. The other two change what you should check before you ship. Here is what actually landed, and what a small team running automations should do about it.

Microsoft MAI-Code-1.1-Flash

Microsoft shipped an upgraded coding model into GitHub Copilot at one quarter the price of the version it launched in June.

Why it matters: pricing is now $0.20 per million input tokens, $0.02 cached, and $1.20 output, against $0.75, $0.075 and $4.50 for MAI-Code-1-Flash. Microsoft also reports 25 percent fewer tokens to finish the same task and 25 percent faster streaming, so the efficiency gain compounds with the price cut rather than sitting beside it. Benchmarks moved too: 22 percent better on Terminal-Bench 2.1 through the Copilot CLI and 15 percent on .NET tasks, plus native vision for reading images inside a coding workflow. Annual Copilot subscribers get a 0.25x premium request multiplier. One date belongs in your calendar: the older MAI-Code-1-Flash is removed from every Copilot surface on September 10, 2026, so anything pinned to it needs a new target before then.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning and NeMo Switchyard

NVIDIA released a 30B mixture-of-experts open model built for long-running agents, alongside a routing library that decides which model handles each step.

Why it matters: the model is free to download, use and modify, with no NVIDIA licence and no fee, and it runs on a single GPU in an ordinary workstation. NVIDIA aims it squarely at the repetitive work inside an agent loop — code review, tool calls, security alert triage, billing questions — claiming output up to four times faster than comparable models and agent tasks finishing roughly 30 percent quicker. Switchyard is the half that touches your bill: it routes each request to a model sized for that step, and NVIDIA's internal testing puts the cost at about one third of running Opus 4.8 alone while holding accuracy near frontier level. These are vendor numbers on a vendor benchmark, so treat them as a hypothesis to test on your own workload, not a result to quote.

DeepSeek V4 Pro (0813)

DeepSeek promoted V4 Pro out of preview overnight, with a one-million-token context window and Codex support.

Why it matters: maximum output is 384K tokens, concurrency is 500, and the model now speaks the Responses API and Anthropic API format alongside OpenAI's. In practice that means an agent already built against Claude can be pointed at it with a configuration change instead of a rewrite. Agent scores moved a long way from the preview build: Terminal Bench 2.1 from 72.1 to 87.9, DeepSWE from 12.8 to 62.7. Pricing did not change with the promotion and remains three times V4-Flash per token, but DeepSeek flagged on August 6 that a broad API price rise is planned and expected to be large. If you are midway through an evaluation, the cost numbers you collect this week may not be the numbers you pay next month.

LTX-2.5

LTX, spun out of Lightricks, released its open-weights video and world model on Hugging Face with native ComfyUI integration.

Why it matters: organisations under $10 million in annual recurring revenue can use it free, and larger companies negotiate a licence. Self-hosted on two GB200 chips it produced a 10-second 720p clip in 6.8 seconds, faster than playing the clip back, and it won 67 percent of quality blind tests. Most readers do not have GB200s, so the more useful figures are the distilled build running on RTX GPUs and Macs, and the hosted LTX-2.5 Fast tier at $0.09 per second, which works out to $0.90 for a 10-second clip with audio. For anyone producing repetitive short video for listings, ads or product pages, that is a per-asset cost you can plan around rather than a subscription you hope to use enough of.

Anthropic watermarks Claude output worldwide

Anthropic confirmed that Claude models released on or after August 2, 2026 weave an imperceptible watermark into generated text, applied globally rather than only in the EU that required it.

Why it matters: coverage spans the Platform API, Claude, Claude Code, Claude Cowork and Claude Tag, and it carries through cloud partners including AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. Generated .svg, .png and .jpg files instead get signed C2PA provenance metadata. The detail worth reading twice is Anthropic's own disclosure: a detected mark shows the text passed through Claude, not that Claude wrote it. Ask the model to proofread, translate or summarise your own writing and the output carries the mark regardless of where the substance came from. Detection tools have not shipped, older models are still being retrofitted, and the EU grace period closes December 2, 2026. Nothing breaks for you this week — but what you start recording this week is the part you control.

Automation tip of the day

Log where AI touched each deliverable, before anyone else decides for you. The watermark story makes an abstract worry concrete: the mark proves processing, not authorship, and it will land on work that is entirely yours if you ran it through a model for grammar or translation. Add one column to whatever list you already use to track deliverables, with three values: none, assist, draft. Assist means formatting, grammar or translating your own words. Draft means the model produced the substance. It takes about five seconds per item, needs no tooling, and it is the only account of your own process that you own. If a client, a platform or an editor ever asks, an answer written before the question is worth considerably more than one assembled after it.

Worth a look

Notice who cut prices today and who warned about raising them. Microsoft took its coding model to a quarter of the old price, NVIDIA gave away a 30B agent model with no licence required, LTX made its video model free below $10 million ARR. Every one of those companies sells something else that the cheap thing feeds: Copilot seats, GPUs, an enterprise licence later. DeepSeek, which mostly sells tokens, is the one signalling a large increase. That is a pattern rather than a verdict — small, fast, strategically subsidised lanes will likely keep getting cheaper, and raw frontier capacity likely will not. The defensive move is boring and costs nothing: make switching models a configuration change instead of a project. That is precisely why V4 Pro shipping Anthropic-format and Responses API compatibility matters more than its benchmark table. So give yourself one question to answer today. If your main model doubled in price tomorrow, is moving off it one line, one afternoon, or one quarter? If you cannot answer that, finding out is this week's work, not next quarter's.
