AI Nexus Daily — November 12, 2026

Practical AI-automation news that saves you time and money. No hype, just what ships and what it costs.

This week the story is clear: the execution tier of AI just got dramatically cheaper, and open tooling is removing lock-in. Here are five findings worth acting on today.

ModelMeta Muse Ember: a $0.10/$0.50 execution model that costs ~1/3 per task of Haiku 5.5

Released Nov 9, Muse Ember is Meta's smallest, most efficient model, built for the repetitive steps agents take all day — tool calls, routing, data extraction, long-document parsing. It lists at $0.10 in / $0.50 out per million tokens (same as Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna) but Meta claims it hits Haiku 5.5's benchmark score at roughly a third of the cost per finished task, thanks to cheaper cached input ($0.004 vs $0.01) and a 200K-token threshold before rates step up (Haiku steps up at 100K). It carries a 1M-token context and reads text, images, audio and video. The pattern to copy: "Muse Wonder plans, Muse Ember handles the volume" — one frontier model for reasoning, a cheap model for the thousands of small steps.

CheapestAmazon Nova Micro is now the cheapest model per task for classification, support and RAG

Verified Nov 4–10 across published list prices, Nova Micro wins the per-month cost race for the high-volume workloads automations actually run: high-volume classification (~$2.03/mo), customer-support chat (~$5.77/mo) and RAG answers (~$3.50/mo) — all below Nova Lite, Qwen Flash and Claude Haiku 5.5. At $0.035 in / $0.14 out per million tokens, it is the right default for routing, tagging and extraction loops where quality is "good enough" and volume is brutal. Rule of thumb: send the boring 90% of calls to the cheapest model that passes your eval.

Open-weightGLM 4.7 Flash: $0.06/$0.40, open-weight and tool-call capable

Pricing verified Nov 8, GLM 4.7 Flash (Z.ai) is the cheapest capable automation model at $0.06 in / $0.40 out per million tokens — about $1.40/month at 10M input + 2M output tokens. It is open-weight with working tool calls, so you can start on a hosted API and move to your own GPUs later without rewriting prompts. For classification, extraction and routing at scale, it undercuts every proprietary option. Keep a hosted fallback, but the open-weight path means your bill can trend toward zero as you self-host.

Open sourceFriday lets you run Claude Code and Codex as a local crew — no vendor lock-in

Released Nov 1, Friday (github.com/splashxmoon/friday) is an open-source orchestration layer that runs Claude Code and Codex as collaborative crews on your own machine. You assign specialized roles — one agent for schema/architecture, another for boilerplate/UI, a third for tests — and Friday handles inter-agent messaging, shared context and task handoffs. It runs entirely local (only the model API calls leave your box), is config-driven, and supports custom roles. Takeaway: multi-agent orchestration no longer requires a paid cloud control plane; wire your own crew and keep full control of the workflow.

ScaleAnthropic Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2: 1,000+ sub-agents built a bootable OS in 8 days — and got cheaper

Released Nov 9, Fable 5.2 coordinated over 1,000 sub-agents to build a working OS from an empty folder in eight days (kernel, drivers, file save, window display, login), and converted Fermat's Last Theorem into a computer-verifiable form in ~30 hours. Pricing dropped: input $10→$8 and output $50→$40 per million, with up to 30% faster responses. Mythos 5.2 lifts restrictions for reviewed orgs. The lesson for builders: the orchestrator-plus-specialist-herd pattern is now proven at extreme scale — design your agents as one planner fanning out to many cheap workers.

Automation tip of the day: Build a four-layer cost defense for every agent you ship. (1) Model ladder — route bulk execution (routing, extraction, classification) to the cheapest model that passes your eval: Nova Micro ($0.035/$0.14), GLM 4.7 Flash ($0.06/$0.40) or Muse Ember ($0.10/$0.50) — and reserve frontier models for planning only. (2) Prompt/context caching so repeated calls don't re-pay for the same context (Ember's $0.004 cached input is 2.5x cheaper than Haiku's). (3) A per-task router (Weave-style) that auto-picks the right model per call. (4) A kill switch with an approval gate on high-impact actions. Blend the ladder with a router and your cost drops while quality holds.
