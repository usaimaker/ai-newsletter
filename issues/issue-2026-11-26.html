AI Automation Daily - November 26, 2026

Today's theme: route the work, not the whole workflow to one expensive model. Five fresh developments that cut the real cost of an automation by moving routine steps to cheaper or local executors, removing per-conversation fees, and demanding human approval before an agent spends money. All vendor savings figures below are claims from the vendors or their users, not independently audited results.

1. Multi-model orchestration: route the routine 80 percent to a cheap executor

A widely shared breakdown of the open-source macOS agent Fazm makes a simple economic point about desktop automation. In a typical session roughly 80 percent of tokens go to routine operations: reading element labels, confirming a button position, executing a click. Those tokens were being billed at the same per-token rate as the 20 percent that actually required planning. The proposed fix is a controller, usually a strong reasoning model, that decomposes the task and assigns each step to the cheapest model able to do it reliably, stepping in only for an unexpected dialog, a CAPTCHA or a page that loaded differently. Two practical routing patterns: by task type (vision to a fast vision model, planning to the strong model, execution to the cheapest tool-call-capable model), or confidence-based, starting cheap and escalating on a low-confidence answer. If your agent bill is dominated by repetitive steps, this is the first change to test, and it costs nothing but a routing table.

2. Salesforce gives SMBs a free Agentforce Coworker tier

Announced November 25, 2026, eligible Salesforce Starter, Pro and Enterprise customers now get a foundational Agentforce Coworker at no extra cost, where the previous consumption model started around 2 dollars per conversation. The assistant handles inbound questions, lead qualification and internal admin work, grounded in the customer's own Data Cloud, with prebuilt templates for appointment scheduling and basic case resolution and a fixed monthly allotment of autonomous credits before standard rates apply. The Einstein Trust Layer is meant to keep customer data out of model training, and the coworker is integrated with Slack so people can take over a thread when the agent reaches its limit. Rollout starts in North America with global availability expected by the end of Q1 2027. Practical move: if you already pay per conversation for a CRM bot, check whether your edition qualifies and benchmark the free credit pool against last month's bill before renewing anything.

3. One open-source maintainer cut agentic coding spend by 98 percent

Frank Rousseau documented ten months of agent-driven work on the Kitsu open-source project: about 600 dollars a month for three Claude Code Ultra subscriptions, covering roughly 70 billion tokens, against an estimated 30,000 dollars of equivalent API usage. The output was not trivial. A Vue.js frontend was migrated from Options API to Composition API across 300 components with responsive redesign, a Python backend gained Pydantic validation and strict OpenAPI specs, 6,500 frontend tests were added, and parallel agent sessions handled data migration, bug fixes and a Tauri desktop app. Open issues fell from 70 to under 10. The method was iterative short prompts plus a Claude.md file holding constraints and links to lint rules, rather than large static specifications. The number is self-reported and the subscription comparison is not apples-to-apples with metered API use, but the lesson is sound: for a sustained heavy workload, a flat-rate subscription with disciplined context rules can beat a usage bill by an order of magnitude. Model the comparison on your own task volume.

4. Pine Labs ships SMB agents that ask before they spend

On November 25, 2026 Pine Labs launched Guide and Marketer Agent inside its Pine Labs One merchant app, built on its Agentic Org platform with Google Gemini models and rolling out to millions of Indian merchants. Guide answers plain-language questions over store sales, POS transactions, settlements, refunds and payment activations, so a single-owner business does not have to dig through dashboards. Marketer Agent converts a goal stated in everyday language into keyword research, creative, audience targeting and a complete Google campaign, but the merchant reviews and approves the campaign and budget before any money is spent, and the agent then publishes without the merchant touching Google Ads directly. It also tries to match ad interactions to actual in-store transactions. A governance layer called Grantex controls tool and data access. An Agent App Store is planned, starting in Singapore and reaching India by December 15, 2026. Practical move: copy the approval-before-spend pattern even if you never use this product, since it is the cheapest guardrail against an agent spending a budget on the wrong goal.

5. Qwen Go opens free agent phone calls

Also on November 25, Alibaba's personal agent Qwen Go opened a call-on-behalf service. After the user states the recipient and the goal and grants authorization, the agent places the call to book a restaurant room, ask a hotel about lost property or confirm a courier delivery, then reports the outcome. Scheduled calls are supported. This is a time-saving step rather than a model benchmark: the agent moves from text and browser tasks into a real-world phone conversation with a closed loop from intent to reported result. The hard part is not speech recognition but intent understanding, dialogue strategy and reliable result feedback. Practical move: pick one recurring phone chore in your business, let an agent handle it in a controlled pilot, and measure minutes recovered per week. Keep a human on the line for anything involving payment or a binding commitment.

Automation tip of the day: route by complexity and gate the spend

Do not send every step of a workflow to the model that writes your strategy. (1) Split the loop into plan, execute and verify, and price each stage separately. (2) Send repetitive, well-defined execution steps to the cheapest model that can reliably emit a tool call, or to a local model where confidentiality or volume makes per-token fees painful. (3) Use a free trial window to benchmark a candidate on your own workload, then keep a logged escalation rate so a cheap router cannot quietly fail. (4) Put a human approval gate and a hard per-run cap in front of any agent that can publish, purchase or call someone. (5) Judge every vendor savings claim by cost per completed task on your data, not by tokens or benchmark scores.
