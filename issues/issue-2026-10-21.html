Cheap Inference and Hands-Off Tasks: AI Automation Picks for October 21, 2026

The launches this week share one theme: the price of doing AI work keeps dropping, while a new class of tools lets you hand off whole jobs instead of single prompts. Whether you want a smaller token bill or fewer manual steps, there is something here you can apply this week. Five items worth your attention.

Claude Haiku 5.5 cuts high-volume costs to $0.10 per million

Anthropic shipped Haiku 5.5 on October 7 at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 output (up to 100k-token prompts), with cache reads at $0.01. It is positioned for summaries, classification, database queries, and customer support triage.

Why it matters: any support or document-tagging automation you scoped this year just got far cheaper to run. If you priced a ticket-routing or extraction pipeline against a frontier model, re-run the arithmetic against Haiku 5.5 before renewing — high-volume jobs are where the savings compound.

ScaleDown CDMs are 467x cheaper than frontier LLMs

ScaleDown launched Constrained Decoding Models on October 14: task-specific models for classification, extraction, and summarization that skip token-by-token generation and deliver 200x faster, 467x cheaper output than frontier models. Pricing is $0.05 per million input tokens with free output, 1M-token multimodal context, and 50M free tokens for new accounts in the first 50 hours.

Why it matters: most business automations end in a pick-or-extract step, not a write step. Routing that step to a CDM instead of a general model can cut the bill by two to three orders of magnitude while keeping structured, predictable output. This is the single biggest cost lever in this issue.

DeepSeek opens Search and Fetch APIs free until October 31

DeepSeek made its in-house Search and Fetch APIs available to all developers at $0.75 per 1,000 calls, with free access for API users through October 31, 2026. The same window covers the V4.1-Flash-1017 model on Hugging Face and ModelScope.

Why it matters: if you build agents that need live web context or page fetching, the next ten days are effectively free. Stand up a retrieval or monitoring workflow now, capture the integration, and you only start paying once the trial closes. Set a calendar reminder so the bill does not surprise you in November.

AutoTask AI runs multi-step workflows with error recovery

AutoTask AI, launched this month, takes a task in plain language, breaks it into subtasks, assigns each to an integrated tool (calendar, email, CRM, project management), executes it, and reports back. The differentiator is the error-recovery layer: a failed subtask gets a resolution attempt before escalating to you. There are 47 native integrations and an open API; a free tier covers 10 tasks per month, Pro from $25.

Why it matters: the value is not the integrations, it is the refusal to stall. Most automation breaks the moment one step fails; AutoTask keeps moving and only pings you when genuinely stuck. That turns a brittle script into something you can actually leave running unattended.

The OpenAI Decisions API answers in 150ms for input-token price

The OpenAI Decisions API, in public beta since October 6, chooses from answers you define in advance instead of generating text, running on GPT-6 Luna at about 150ms per decision — up to 10x faster than Luna through the Responses API. You pay only for input tokens at $0.10 per million.

Why it matters: for routing, approval, or any yes/no-or-from-list decision, a generator is overkill. Pinning the answer set and calling Decisions turns a slow, costly, hallucination-prone step into a fast, cheap, deterministic one. Combine it with a CDM and most of your pipeline never touches a frontier model at all.

Automation tip of the day

Split your pipeline into a decide-or-extract half and a write half, then run the first half on cheap, constrained models. Today's releases make this almost free: Haiku 5.5 for light drafting, ScaleDown CDMs for extraction, OpenAI Decisions for routing — reserve a frontier model for the one step that actually needs open-ended reasoning. Same result, a fraction of the cost, and far fewer surprise bills.

Issue of October 21, 2026. Verify each announcement against its primary source and do your own diligence before committing budget or changing a workflow.
