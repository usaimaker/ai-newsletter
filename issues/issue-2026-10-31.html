Issue 2026-10-31 · practical AI tools that save time and money

Five fresh, hands-on picks from this week's launches - each one chosen for a concrete money- or time-saving angle you can act on today.

Oct 27Replit Cloud Agents let non-engineers ship tested PRs

Replit opened Cloud Agents to Pro and Enterprise users on Oct 27. A designer, support rep, or PM describes a change in one sentence, and the agent finds the relevant code, edits it to team style, opens the running app in an isolated sandbox, clicks through the flow like a user, fixes failures, and attaches screenshots/video to the pull request. Reviewers get proof it runs, not a text description. Internal use cut token spend 51% and dropped a routine data task from 16 minutes to 1. Why it saves money: business stakeholders stop queuing behind engineers for small, verified changes.

Oct 26GPT-6.1 Terra halves OpenAI's agent API price

OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra mid-tier model lands at $1 / $5 per million tokens (cached input $0.05/M) - half of GPT-6.1 Sol - while keeping visual reasoning, computer-use, and writing. It is live in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API. Why it saves money: for high-volume agent loops, routing bulk reasoning and tool-calling to Terra instead of the flagship can cut API bills roughly in half with little quality loss on routine tasks.

Oct 19Inception Mercury 3: a 1,068 tok/s diffusion LLM at $0.10/M

Mercury 3 is a diffusion LLM that generates tokens in parallel, claiming 1,068 tokens/sec on NVIDIA GPUs with parity to GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 (vendor claims). It is OpenAI-API compatible and available via Inception, OpenRouter, Baseten, and Vercel. Launch pricing is $0.10/M input and $0.35/M output (50% discount), and new keys get 100M free tokens. Why it saves money: real-time voice and browser agents stay responsive and cheap, and the free-token grant covers a full pilot.

Oct 7IrisGo AI OS watches your workflow, then repeats it

IrisGo (backed by Andrew Ng's AI Fund, #1 Product Hunt on Oct 7) is a desktop "AI OS" for Windows 11 and Apple Silicon Macs. Its Watch & Learn feature records you doing a multi-step admin job once (filing expenses, pulling a weekly report), drafts the next run for your approval, then repeats it on new inputs automatically. It also ships email triage and a daily priorities brief. Why it saves time: free during public beta, it automates the repetitive desktop busywork no SaaS ever quite captured.

Rolling outClaude Duet runs as a proactive always-on agent

Anthropic's Claude Duet operates continuously across connected apps - email, calendar, and productivity tools - and keeps working after you close the app. It drafts replies, schedules meetings, and flags urgent items on its own, learning your priorities over time. Why it saves time: it shifts from reactive chat to proactive management, surfacing deadlines and conflicts before you ask, cutting the cognitive tax of staying on top of everything.

Automation tip of the day

Route your high-volume steps to the cheapest model and keep a kill switch. Build every agent loop with a model ladder: send classification, extraction, and bulk drafting to the cheapest capable model (Mercury 3 at $0.10/M or GPT-6.1 Terra at $1/M), and reserve the flagship only for the hard reasoning step. Cap each run with a per-run token budget and a hard stop, and cache repeated prompt prefixes so cached input drops to $0.05/M. The 51% token cut Replit reported came largely from exactly this discipline - not from a smarter model.
