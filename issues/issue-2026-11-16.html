Issue 2026-11-16 · Practical AI tools that save money and time

Five fresh, real findings from November 10–16, 2026 — cheaper agent models, a free local agent, and hard numbers on what a small model can replace.

1. GLM 5.3 is now the cheapest strong agent model (Nov 16 leaderboard)

The November 16 Agents leaderboard puts open-weight GLM 5.3 at 53.1 points — within five points of the $10/$50-per-million leader Claude Fable 5.1 — at just $0.07 input / $1.40 output per million. Its Flash sibling, GLM 5.3 Flash, is $0.04/$0.078. At a typical 1M input + 250K output tokens a month, that is about $0.42 instead of tens of dollars for the same class of agent work. The takeaway: route bulk agent steps to an open-weight model and keep the flagship only for the hard planning calls.

2. Revolut cut AI coding cost ~90% by self-hosting open models (reported Nov 16)

A November 16 industry report details how fintech Revolut replaced OpenAI and Anthropic coding assistants with self-hosted Zhipu GLM for roughly 2,000 engineers, running on 56 B200 GPUs. Average coding time fell about 60%, AI spend dropped close to 90%, and blended inference cost landed near $0.30 per million tokens — about a tenth of the prior bill. This is the clearest real-world proof yet that cost-sensitive, high-volume agent work (coding, batch processing, terminal automation) is moving to open weights.

3. n8n ships Nod: a free, open-source agent that runs on your computer (Nov 10)

n8n Labs released Nod, a research-preview personal agent under Apache 2.0. It has its own sandboxed browser, works in connected apps and on sites with no integration, and handles recurring admin — chasing invoices, downloading statements, cancelling trials, pulling dashboard numbers into a report. It scored 93.2% on LongMemEval for long-term memory. Cost controls are baked in: a receipt for every task, a monthly spending cap, and a check-in once a task passes $5. The app is free; you only pay your model provider, or connect a free local model.

4. A $97 fine-tune made a 27B open model beat Sonnet at one-sixth the cost (Nov 16)

Ops company Komodor pointed its self-improving loop at the model itself. Using Qwen3.5-27B's own investigation sessions, a teacher model corrected the first wrong turn and only higher-scoring runs were kept. Four LoRA jobs on Together AI cost $27; the whole cycle cost $97. On 40 Kubernetes root-cause tasks the score jumped from 0.287 to 0.871 at $0.14 per run versus $0.84 for Sonnet 4.6. Caveat worth copying: it was trained and scored on the same incidents, so unseen cases are the real test. The lesson — harness and model are one optimization problem, and a frontier model's best job is often writing the data a small model learns from.

5. Pixeltree: a 19 MB local model that turns screenshots into UI text (Nov 16)

Developer SentimentalK229 released Pixeltree, a 19 MB local model that converts any screenshot into a text list of UI elements. Opus labelled 2,782 screenshots under one convention, a small YOLO model was fine-tuned, and it reached F1 0.854 on ten Linux apps it had never seen. Cheap models benefit most: GLM 5.3 Flash gained 15 points, and a text-only model completed 33 of 48 desktop tasks through it. Code, weights and the labelling recipe are public. If your agents burn tokens on screenshots, a small local "screen-to-text" front end is now a viable, free accelerator.

Automation tip of the day — Build a model ladder with receipts. Route the bulk of your agent steps to a cheap open-weight model (GLM 5.3 Flash at $0.04/$0.078 per M), reserve the flagship for planning and grading, and wrap any action-taking agent in a per-task receipt, a monthly cap and an approval gate (as Nod does). Every few weeks, tune the harness and the small model together — Komodor's $97 cycle shows a narrow-task fine-tune can replace a frontier model at a sixth of the price. Test on unseen cases before you trust the number.
